# Revised main figure captions

Fig. 1. How evaluation choices change the interpretation of one spatial selection. (a) Five schematic tiles are ordered by risk, with the first four retained. Blue pixels denote reference hazard, light pixels valid background and gray pixels invalid area. Solid frames mark retained tiles; the dashed frame marks the deferred tile. (b) The same retained tiles can be evaluated by averaging tile losses or by pooling their confusion matrices before computing loss; the included class set is stated separately. (c) In this equal-area-pixel illustration, the retained set covers 4/5 tiles (80.0%), 232/296 valid pixels (78.4%) and 36/72 hazard pixels (50.0%). These are illustrative counts, not experimental observations. Tile scale and zoning are fixed. The empirical analyses are post hoc; development provenance and the scope of validation are specified in Sections 2.1 and 3.5.

Fig. 2. Ranking effects depend on the evaluation functional and coverage region. (a) Ridge-minus-stored-self-overlap AURC for unchanged calibration-event orderings under four functionals; each model has 33 correlated held-event diagnostics. (b) Test v2-minus-stored-self-overlap AURC under event-pooled mIoU and foreground (FG) endpoints; each model has nine correlated fold-seed runs. Small points show every paired effect; diamonds and whiskers show means ± sample SD. (c,d) Coverage-specific paired risk differences in P1 and P2, respectively; lines are means and shading spans the 25th–75th percentiles across the nine runs. The vertical line marks nominal 0.80 tile-count coverage. Negative differences favor ridge/v2. Dispersion is descriptive, not a confidence interval. These post-hoc audits use the original stored binary comparator; exact-SDC and target-matched revision analyses are reported separately in Tables S15–S17.

Fig. 3. Error alignment and foreground-abundance dependence. (a) Within-event Spearman correlations with tile loss or predicted foreground fraction (FG). Each small point averages three seed-specific correlations for one event; diamonds average the 11 event values per model. (b) Spearman correlations across the same 11 seed-averaged events between v2-minus-stored-self-overlap AURC and hazard-coverage differences. At nominal 0.80 tile coverage, the two-sided uncorrected p values are 0.312 (P1) and 0.083 (P2); for integrated hazard coverage they are 0.110 and 0.035. These four exploratory tests are not multiplicity-adjusted. (c) Event-macro pooled-mIoU AURC for v2, stored self-overlap and directional abundance baselines. Points show all nine correlated runs per block; diamonds and whiskers show mean ± sample SD. All panels are post hoc. Events share fitted models across rotations, and neither seed averaging nor descriptive whiskers establishes independent replication.

Fig. 4. Equal tile-count coverage retained unequal hazard content. (a,b) Reference-hazard coverage for v2 at nominal 80% tile coverage in P1 and P2. Dots are event means over three seeds; horizontal whiskers span the observed seed minimum and maximum. Black vertical ticks show realized tile-count coverage and the dotted line marks the nominal target. (c,d) Mean hazard-coverage curves for v2, stored binary self-overlap and abundance baselines across 33 correlated event-run records per block. The diagonal denotes count-proportional hazard retention. Coverage is retained reference-hazard pixels divided by the full-event reference-hazard count. These are post-hoc descriptive comparisons; seed ranges are not confidence intervals.

Fig. 5. Block-level effects and multiclass evaluation sensitivity. (a) Paired event-macro AURC differences for v2 versus the original stored self-overlap comparator. Points show all nine correlated runs in each primary block and all three seed runs in each external block; diamonds are block means. E1 uses the custom multiclass self-overlap extension. External coefficients and thresholds were fitted using labeled target-domain calibration data. (b) Kuro Siwo fixed-minus-present class-set risk for unchanged v2 and custom-self-overlap orderings, averaged across three seeds. Shading marks coverage 0.10–0.50 inclusive, which accounts for 83.3% of the integrated mean shift; the shift averaged across both orderings is 0.0121 at nominal 0.80 coverage. All analyses are post hoc, and repeated seeds do not provide independent external validation.
